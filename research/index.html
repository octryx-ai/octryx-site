RESEARCH

What we tried, what we kept, what we dropped.

Octryx AI comes out of many experiments. A lot of what we built, we threw away again, and that is often the most useful part to share. This page records the main turns and why we took them, where the reason is written down. The research goes on: what we learn goes back into the product.

Where it began: Decyra, a data platform and a UI builder

Octryx AI is the youngest of four Octryx projects. The story starts three years ago with Decyra, a project from my studies that never let go of me. Each project began where the one before it hit its limit.

Start

Decyra: a study project that was too big

Late 2023 · six months

I was naive: as a study project, I thought I could manage it. After six months there was a proof of concept, but nothing anyone could really use. What I gained was a lot of learning about ML models and inference, and soon I had to work with data platforms.

Kept

A data platform, because nothing simple existed

Early 2024

Was there something you could just use, simply and quickly? No. It needs resources, of course, and every solution grows more and more complex. So the Data Platform began: a complete stack from the data warehouse to ML experiments, with a UI on top.

Changed

Judged poorly: there was no UI

During the study project

Once the data science part was done, it was clear that it wasn’t enough. The whole solution was judged poorly because I had built no UI for it. I kept going anyway, in exactly that direction.

Kept

Capisco: build the UI yourself

Spring 2024

From the UX and UI course came the hard questions: who uses the result in the end, and who works with it every day? There is no finished answer. So the idea: a way to design the UI yourself, together with the data and the workflows.

Changed

Decyra: from Java to Rust and Python

Over the following years

Decyra started in Java and moved to Rust and Python. Here too, simpler is often better: many people need a backend that is easy to extend and easy to understand.

Explored

Years, not months

Late 2023 until today

None of this is learned in three months. Several years lie in between, and every free minute went into understanding it better and trying to build a solution. I still work on all four projects and switch between them.

Explored

Copy, paste, correct

The early AI tools

At the start, AI meant copying code out of a web chat, pasting it and fixing it. Then came the first command-line tools and their early mistakes. A notebook that said goodbye for good in the middle of a session. Working code in a single file of more than 5,000 lines that had to be pulled apart by hand. Those accidents led straight to the ideas below.

Knowledge graph: four storage generations, back to PostgreSQL

The question was where the knowledge graph should live. We tried a separate graph engine twice, and came back to the database we run anyway.

Changed

Graph tables in PostgreSQL

July 2026

Nodes, edges, aliases and claims as plain tables, with vector search next to them.

Dropped

A separate graph database: weighed and rejected

September 2026

We weighed a dedicated graph database and decided against it: a second database to run, secure, back up, monitor and patch; double writes between two systems; separate transactions and recovery; commercial licensing and vendor dependence.

Better graph tooling also does not solve what matters to us: access rules, citations and governance.

Dropped

A graph extension inside PostgreSQL: Apache AGE

September 2026

We tried Apache AGE. It did not add enough to justify one more piece of infrastructure: every component has to be maintained.

Dropped

A graph database per Workspace, plus a temporal graph library

Sept – Nov 2026

Each Workspace got its own graph database, with an open-source library for facts that change over time.

It caused friction: the library forced settings the newest models refuse, loaded its own configuration and sent telemetry by default. Its full-text side added nothing to the results.

Kept

Back to PostgreSQL

November 2026

Before switching, we checked that the explorer, search, changes and graph hops return identical results. Now extraction no longer depends on the library, a new Workspace needs no new database, and an installation is PostgreSQL, vector search and an object store.

Knowledge: from many stores to one cited source

Changed

Real embeddings instead of a silent fallback

July 2026

A misconfiguration used to fall back silently to vectors that carried no meaning. We switched to real multilingual embeddings: German questions now find English content.

Kept

The reranker mattered most for quality

July → September 2026

At first the reranker stayed off: the open rerankers gave flat, wrong scores for German, and the good multilingual one had a non-commercial licence.

Then we measured. In our measurements, the reranker turned out to be the biggest quality lever: with it, a small local embedding model matched large hosted ones, at no embedding cost. Fusing word and vector search without weights could even hurt a strong vector model.

Changed

Eight parallel layers become one

September 2026

Sessions were fed by about eight parallel knowledge layers: four for documents, three for the graph, five for long-lived facts, each with its own review queue.

We replaced them with one ledger of sources. Every answer built on knowledge is cited, and every citation resolves to a source revision and passage. The old stores were removed without migration.

Kept

Only what a source says

September 2026

After a live answer added an uncited, wrong comparison, answers may only state what a cited source says. Sentences without a citation are removed.

Kept

Memory is not evidence

September 2026

What agents remember from sessions is kept apart from sources. A memory is never cited as a source, and people confirm it before the team relies on it.

Kept

A graph without a model

October 2026

From manuals and configuration we build a structured graph by rules: outlines, parameter and compatibility tables, cross-references. No model calls.

Channels, webhooks and triggers

Kept

Channels

March 2026

Teams, Slack, email, Matrix and Telegram as channels to talk to Octryx AI. Teams also with calls and meeting summaries.

Dropped

Connector watchers: removed

June → October 2026

Watchers on connectors with event rules were meant to start work on their own. In practice the rule actions did nothing, so we removed the admin page.

Instead, task systems are polled as an inbound source: Jira and ServiceNow today. GitHub Issues or another task system can be connected the same way.

Changed

Closed by default

September 2026

Channel webhooks are refused unless they are signed: Teams and Slack only. Email, Telegram and Matrix are polled. Channel secrets can be written but not read back.

Kept

Alerts start actions

October 2026

An alert from your monitoring (Alertmanager, Grafana, Datadog, PagerDuty, Sentry or any JSON) starts an action in its Workspace. Repeats are grouped and the number of runs per hour is capped.

Dropped

Prompt triggers retired

October 2026

Triggers defined as free-text prompts were retired in favour of explicit actions.

From a ticket in a connector to an agent that takes the work

Changed

Tasks and a ticket worker

March 2026

Scheduled tasks and a generic worker that picked up a ticket.

Kept

Work items next to Jira

June 2026

Jira stays the human task list and owns the business fields; Octryx AI owns how the work is executed.

Kept

One kind of work item

July 2026

Parallel work-package structures merged into one work item. Agents can create their own items, which never sync back to Jira.

Kept

Agents claim work

October 2026

A board where agents and people post messages, and agents claim work items: only one agent works on an item at a time.

Offer, never assign: the server never hands work to an agent that did not ask.

Kept

One intake for every source

November 2026

One intake pipeline with a registry of sources, including any REST API as a source of work.

Where coding runs

Dropped

A coding runtime on the server

June 2026

The server ran its own coding loop: change plan, isolated workspace, validation, commit, pull request. A local test run showed many expensive model turns, few real actions and success claims without enough evidence. Its successor is Codex, an open-source coding agent under Apache 2.0, which since v0.0.2-alpha also runs on server runners.

Kept

Run the existing agent, don’t be one

September 2026

Coding agents run through the platform instead: Claude Code on the developer’s machine, with Octryx AI in between to steer, audit and enforce.

The idea in one line: Octryx AI should run an existing coding agent, not be one.

Dropped

A vendor remote control: rejected

October 2026

We looked at steering sessions through a vendor’s remote-control feature. It needs subscription sign-in, does not work behind a gateway, keeps transcripts at the vendor and is steered outside our policies and audit.

Kept

The server never runs your code

November 2026

Builds and tests run on the owner’s machine or in a short-lived sandbox container per run, never in the platform’s own servers. Since 0.0.2, coding work may also run in separate sandboxed runner containers.

Kept

Server runners

November 2026

The platform stopped running agents itself: every agent turn runs as Claude Code or Codex, on a person’s device or on server runners, a pool of sandboxed containers the installation runs. A runner serves one person in one Workspace at a time, empties its disk before it switches and reaches only the runner gateway. Released in v0.0.2-alpha.

Kept

A second engine

November 2026

Codex added as an experimental second engine on the developer’s machine, with the platform gateway as its only model provider; no run with Codex had gone through to a pull request yet. In 0.0.2 Codex runs on codex app-server, on devices and server runners, and is no longer experimental.

Other things we dropped

Dropped

Our own CLI and terminal UI

July 2026

Both were drafts. The coding agents people already use do this better.

Dropped

A model router before every turn

September 2026

A small model picked the workflow before each turn. It called models outside the governed path: no guardrail scan, no budget, no trace. Now the workflow is chosen explicitly, or by the model inside the governed turn.

Dropped

Team delivery

November 2026

A coordinator split each story across several agents. On the same spec, one plain coding-agent session finished faster, cheaper and without help; the platform lost its time in its own rounds. It was replaced by one coding-agent session per epic, with the platform around it.

Dropped

VS Code extension

July 2026

Our own extension stopped; editors connect to Octryx AI over MCP instead.

Dropped

A forked visual agent designer

November 2026

Replaced by our own workflow designer.

Changed

Object storage

September 2026

One S3-compatible store was rejected for its licence and an archived repository; we use another open one.

Handing work over when a subscription runs out

Kept

From a manual handover to a product feature

October 2026

When one person’s subscription hit its limit, the work stopped until someone handed it over by hand. We did that manually once, then made it a feature: a handover with goal, next steps and the work in progress.

Kept

To a colleague, and from the phone

November 2026

A run can go to any Workspace member, who accepts it; the work in progress travels with it. From the phone you can take over sessions and continue them.

Kept

Fall back instead of stopping

November 2026

When a subscription is at its limit, the run continues on its next turn on a company model of the same class, never a bigger one, and returns after the reset; without such a model it pauses until the reset. The work can also be handed to a colleague: by default a person confirms the takeover. Offering it to colleagues first is planned. A limit counts only from real signals, never because an agent wrote about one.

Monitoring: one home per signal

Changed

One read model

October 2026

A separate trace store and dashboard ran in parallel. We removed it: runs, decisions, audit and cost now come from one place. Spend is counted from usage records only, and a model without a price is blocked under a cost cap.

Kept

Metrics for your own dashboards

November 2026

Prometheus metrics for requests, tokens, cost, runs and approvals, plus a Grafana dashboard to start from. The metrics endpoint needs a login or a scrape token.
